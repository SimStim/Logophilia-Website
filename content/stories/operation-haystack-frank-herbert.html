Lewis Orne walked out of the hospital thirteen months after he was carried in, rebuilt from scraps of himself and the stubborn will to survive. The creche pod had held what remained of his body together; what it could not mend was the quiet fracture beneath his skin, the knowledge that violence had taken pieces of him no surgery could restore. His section chief was waiting with a new assignment. The kind you accept not because you want to, but because the alternative is worse.

Beneath the polished surface of elegant rooms turning slowly toward the moonlight and among old friendships woven through generations, at its center a woman whose presence made the dangerous work of trust feel almost natural, something vast is tightening: an election approaches, and the balance of galactic power hangs by a thread held in unfamiliar hands. In a world where the true battles unfold across dinner tables and in the silences between polished words, the line between ally and enemy dissolves.
