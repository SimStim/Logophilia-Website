The fleet comes out of hot-warp into the murmur of consoles and the faint bite of ozone, a single light swelling in the viewports over a blue planet already counted as taken. The great big guns have never needed a second opinion. Half lit by a screen's glow, Angelica van Manderpootz, attorney at law, greets the armada with a country drawl and the quiet courtesy of a woman who has read the rules of empires more closely than the men who write them.

Behind her stands Earth, a world with no fleet worth the name; before her waits an empire that holds law to be whatever force permits, billions of lives filed away on a ledger that has never once been argued with. Before the guns, though, there will be paperwork. A story about the empire that made the rules, and the woman who read them.
