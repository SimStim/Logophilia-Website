The train carried Brett from Casperton on a summer morning, cornfields sliding past in a slow green pulse, and he watched with the quiet certainty of a man who has outgrown the only world he knows. The tracks ended in a dry field under a sun bleeding across the afternoon. Beyond them was not a city but its silhouette, a facade propped against nothing, its streets populated by figures moving through scenes they had not chosen and could not leave.

The walls were paper-thin. The ground beneath had been hollowed, tunneled, remade into a vast darkness where things lived: translucent and patient, older than anyone remembered. Brett understood, standing in the empty street, that the world he had left might be as thin as this one, that Casperton too could be a shell, that every door might open onto anything at all.
