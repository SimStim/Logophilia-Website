Richard Rowe lives in a world where every word that travels between humans must carry a commercial purpose, must serve the machinery of transaction. Light here is the even glow of indicator panels; sound is the ceaseless hum of automated sorting. He has seen a face that lingers, a woman glimpsed once at a spaceport, and he possesses no sanctioned channel to reach her. So he does something the system has no word for.

The postal system is built for flow: billions of advertising pieces per hour, sorted by machines whose programming includes no category for a letter from one person to another. When Richard’s letter enters that machinery, the machines convulse.
