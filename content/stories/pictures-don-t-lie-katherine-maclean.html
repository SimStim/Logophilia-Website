The radio signals arrived as static at first, a jagged spatter of noise from a constellation no one had thought to question. Joseph Nathen, a decoder who had spent years listening to the chatter between foreign powers, recognized the pattern: not accident but information compressed near the threshold of recognition. He slowed the recordings down, assigned color to the scanning bands on instinct, and watched a window open onto a world that moved at a different speed.

The beings on the screen looked almost human. That was the first assumption, and it would not be the last. As the world’s cameras angled toward an empty airfield and a Senator rehearsed his speech of welcome, the quiet dissonance in the aliens’ movements was a grace that belonged to a different gravity and accumulated toward a revelation no one was prepared to receive. The pictures did not lie. But some distances cannot be seen through a window.
