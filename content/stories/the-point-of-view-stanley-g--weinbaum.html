Dixon Wells had been a test subject for enough of van Manderpootz’s inventions to know every new device hums with its own quiet catastrophe. When the professor unveils the attitudinizor, a contraption that lets one mind see through another’s, Dixon braces for vertigo, for the sting of glimpsing himself under a gaze less merciful than his own. He does not expect to find something beautiful.

But beauty is not in the thing seen but in the seer. Through the attitudinizor’s amber beam, Dixon glimpses a woman as she appears to the man who loves her—a vision so luminous that her actual self, unremarkable in his own sight, becomes a phantom. What he has fallen for is not a person but a perception, and to possess that vision he would have to surrender his own.
