A pulp writer sits in the Tuesday gloom of a restaurant that smells of wine and ash, surrounded by men selling their fancies for a cent a word. They trade shop talk: the slow erosion of writing no one will remember. Then a stranger arrives, tall and awkward, thick glasses catching the light with something deliberate, his fingers working a small metal box that clicks beneath the table.

The story the stranger tells is about a time machine, but its quality is poor; that is not what makes the writer’s certainty fray. What makes him turn as the stranger walks away is a quiet wrongness—too sharp, too precise. At that moment of an ordinary Tuesday, the writer understands that genius does not announce itself.
